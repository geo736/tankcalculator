How the TAN conversion calculator works

Ammonia dissolved in water exists in two forms that are always in balance: un-ionized ammonia (NH₃) and the ammonium ion (NH₄⁺). Most test kits measure both together, called total ammonia. The calculator first converts your reading to total ammonia nitrogen (TAN), the mass of nitrogen in both forms. It then uses pH, temperature and (optionally) salinity to work out what share of that nitrogen is in the un-ionized form, and reports the result in every common way of writing it.

The same chemical amount can be written very differently. 1 mg/L of TAN (as N) is the same water as 1.22 mg/L “total ammonia as NH₃” or 1.29 mg/L “as NH₄⁺”. Mixing these up is one of the most common sources of confusion when comparing a hobby test kit with a lab report or a published guideline.

Why it matters for aquaculture and aquaponics

Fish excrete ammonia through their gills, and in recirculating aquaculture (RAS) and aquaponics it builds up until nitrifying bacteria in the biofilter convert it to nitrite and then nitrate (Timmons & Ebeling, 2010). Un-ionized NH₃ is the form that is toxic to fish because it passes easily across gill membranes; NH₄⁺ is far less toxic (Hargreaves & Tucker, 2004). The toxic share rises roughly tenfold for every one-unit rise in pH, and also rises with temperature. A TAN reading that is harmless at pH 7 can be dangerous at pH 8.5, which is why the same test result must always be read together with pH and temperature.

Formulas

1. Convert the reading to TAN (mg/L as N). Molar masses use the IUPAC standard atomic weights N = 14.007 and H = 1.008, so NH₃ = 17.031 g/mol and NH₄⁺ = 18.039 g/mol.

TAN-N = total NH₃ × 14.007 / 17.031 (= total NH₃ ÷ 1.2159) TAN-N = total NH₄⁺ × 14.007 / 18.039 (= total NH₄⁺ ÷ 1.2879) NH₃ (as NH₃) = NH₃-N × 1.2159 NH₄⁺ (as NH₄⁺) = NH₄-N × 1.2879

2. Find the dissociation constant of ammonium from the water temperature (Emerson et al., 1975), with T in kelvin:

pKa = 0.09018 + 2729.92 / T T = °C + 273.15

3. Salinity correction (optional). Dissolved salts raise the pKa slightly, so a little less of the ammonia is un-ionized in brackish and sea water (Soderberg & Meade, 1991). The calculator uses the ionic-strength correction in the Florida DEP un-ionized ammonia procedure, with salinity S in g/kg (ppt) and t in °C:

I = 19.9273 × S / (1000 − 1.005109 × S) pKa = 0.09018 + 2729.92 / T + (0.1552 − 0.0003142 × t) × I

4. Fraction un-ionized and the split of TAN between the two forms:

f = 1 / (10^(pKa − pH) + 1) NH₃-N = f × TAN-N NH₄-N = (1 − f) × TAN-N

If your kit reports only the free NH₃ (or only NH₄⁺), the calculator runs step 4 in reverse (TAN-N = NH₃-N ÷ f) to recover the total.

Nitrite and nitrate use the same idea, with O = 15.999:

NO₂⁻ = NO₂-N × 46.005 / 14.007 = NO₂-N × 3.284 NO₃⁻ = NO₃-N × 62.004 / 14.007 = NO₃-N × 4.427

Worked example (the default inputs)

A test kit reads 1.0 mg/L TAN (as N) in fresh water at pH 7.5 and 25 °C.

  • T = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K, so pKa = 0.09018 + 2729.92 / 298.15 = 9.2464.
  • f = 1 / (10^(9.2464 − 7.5) + 1) = 1 / (55.77 + 1) = 0.01762, so 1.76% of the ammonia is un-ionized.
  • NH₃-N = 0.01762 × 1.0 = 0.0176 mg/L, and NH₃ = 0.0176 × 1.2159 = 0.0214 mg/L as NH₃.
  • The rest, 0.982 mg/L NH₄-N (1.265 mg/L as NH₄⁺), is ionized ammonium.

0.0214 mg/L NH₃ is just above the conservative 0.02 mg/L long-term target, so the calculator flags it as “caution”. At pH 8.0 the same TAN would give 0.065 mg/L NH₃, inside the range where chronic gill damage has been reported.

For the default nitrite reading, 0.25 mg/L NO₂⁻ = 0.25 ÷ 3.284 = 0.0761 mg/L NO₂-N. For nitrate, 40 mg/L NO₃⁻ = 40 ÷ 4.427 = 9.04 mg/L NO₃-N.

How to read the toxicity status

Tolerance depends strongly on species, life stage, exposure time and other water quality (oxygen, CO₂), so there is no single safe number. The status uses these bands for un-ionized ammonia, in mg/L as NH₃:

NH₃ (mg/L)StatusBasis
below 0.02LowConservative long-term target widely used in fish culture (rule of thumb; coldwater fish such as trout are more sensitive)
0.02 – 0.06CautionAbove the conservative target, below the level where chronic harm is reported in SRAC 463
0.06 – 0.6HarmfulChronic exposure from about 0.06 mg/L can cause gill and kidney damage and reduced growth (Durborow et al., 1997)
0.6 and aboveDangerousShort-term lethal levels for many pond fish begin around 0.6 mg/L (Durborow et al., 1997)

For aquaponics, the FAO manual (Somerville et al., 2014) recommends keeping TAN below about 1 mg/L, which keeps NH₃ low at the near-neutral pH that suits plants and nitrifying bacteria.

Limitations and assumptions

  • The Emerson equation was fitted for fresh water from 0 to 30 °C and is widely used outside that range with small error. The page warns below 0 °C or above 40 °C.
  • The salinity correction is approximate. Published seawater corrections (for example the method in the US EPA 1989 saltwater criteria) differ slightly from each other; with this method, full seawater (35 ppt) gives an un-ionized share about one-fifth lower than fresh water at the same pH and temperature. Salinity is treated as g/kg (ppt ≈ PSU).
  • pH accuracy dominates. An error of 0.1 pH unit changes the NH₃ result by about 25%. Measure pH at the same time and place as ammonia; in ponds pH peaks in the late afternoon.
  • mg/L vs ppm. In fresh water 1 mg/L ≈ 1 ppm. Concentration units here are per litre of water.
  • The toxicity bands are general guidance, not species-specific limits or regulatory criteria.
