How the plant-fish mass ratio calculator works

Aquaponic systems are often described by how many fish they hold per plant or per square metre, but that says little about what the system produces. Colt and Semmens (2022) define plant-fish ratios on numbers (PFR) and on mass (PFRM), and separate two kinds of mass ratio [1]. The standing ratio PFRMt is the weight of plants divided by the weight of fish in the system at a time t, for example at stocking (PFRMo) or at harvest (PFRMh). The experimental ratio PFRMexp is the plant weight gained divided by the fish weight gained over a period, and is the more fundamental measure of system performance [1]. A 2026 lettuce–tilapia study used PFRMexp together with the plant feed conversion ratio to judge six feed loading rates [2].

This page's main result is PFRMexp, from the wet weight gained by the salable plants and by the fish over the same period. A PFRM of 3 means the system grew 3 kg of plants for every kilogram of fish. If you add the feed, it also gives the two feed conversion ratios and checks that their ratio equals PFRM (Eq. 9 of [1]). It compares your PFRM with the theoretical value for your crop and feed. Add the starting weights for the standing ratios, farmgate prices for the revenue ratio, and the grow area, tank volume and period for production per area and per volume and the feed loading rate.

In Plan mode you enter a fish production target, a PFRM and the plant yield your grow beds give per m² over the same period. The page works out the plant production (fish × PFRM), the grow area that needs (plants ÷ yield) and, with a fish FCR, the feed and the loading rate.

Why it matters

Every gram of plant growth in aquaponics comes from nutrients in the fish feed, so plant and fish production are linked through the feed. PFRM puts that link into one number that can be compared between systems, crops and seasons. On a farm it answers a planning question directly: if you want to sell 1 tonne of fish a year, about how much produce, and how much grow area, comes with it? And because plant sales dominate the economics of aquaponics, the revenue ratio built on it, PFRR, shows how much of the income comes from the plants [1].

The theoretical PFRM

The plant FCR has a theoretical value from a nitrogen and a phosphorus mass balance: the feed needed per kg of salable plant when the plants retain an average share of the feed N and P. The element that needs more feed is limiting; its value is the controlling FCRplant (see the plant FCR calculator). Dividing the fish FCR by it gives the controlling PFRM [1]. With the paper's selected values (mean warm- and cold-water feeds, fish FCR 1.40 and 1.10, 22.3% of feed N and 15.1% of feed P retained by the plants), the theoretical PFRM ranged from about 2.5 to 9.1 kg plant per kg fish across 12 crops: lower for nitrogen-rich herbs and greens such as peppermint, basil and parsley, higher for fruiting crops such as tomato and cucumber [1].

Only about 30% of the PFRMexp values the authors could calculate from published aquaponic studies fell inside 2.5–9.1, and only one study reported a PFRM directly [1]. A PFRM well below the theory at the same fish FCR means the plants grew less from the feed than the mass balance predicts. The authors list low nutrient and micronutrient concentrations, suboptimal pH and temperature, and salt build-up as likely causes [1].

Standing ratios and the revenue ratio

The standing ratio changes over a production cycle. Starting from the stocking weights, Eq. 10 of [1] gives it after a feed input FF as PFRMt = (Woplant + FF ÷ FCRplant) ÷ (Wofish + FF ÷ FCRfish): it moves from the stocking ratio towards PFRMexp as more feed goes in, quickly if the two are close [1]. Plant stocking weight is usually small, but fish stocking weight varies widely, so the ratio at harvest can be far below PFRMexp. The plant-fish revenue ratio uses the ratio at harvest: PFRR = PFRMh × (farmgate price of plants ÷ farmgate price of fish), and varied 5–6-fold across the fish-plant combinations in the paper's data [1].

Formulas

With Gplant and Gfish = fresh weight gained over the period, F = feed fed in the same period, dp and df = dry matter (%):

PFRM = Gplant ÷ Gfish (kg plant per kg fish) PFRMdry = (Gplant × dp) ÷ (Gfish × df) FCRfish = F ÷ Gfish FCRplant = F ÷ Gplant FCRfish ÷ FCRplant = (F ÷ Gfish) ÷ (F ÷ Gplant) = Gplant ÷ Gfish = PFRM

The last line is Eq. 9 of [1]: when both FCRs use the same feed over the same period, the feed cancels and their ratio is exactly PFRMexp. It holds only on the same weight basis (both wet or both dry). It is useful in practice: at a given fish FCR, raising PFRM is the same thing as lowering FCRplant.

Theoretical, standing and revenue ratios (Eqs. 2, 10, 11, 16–19 of [1]):

PFRMcontrol = FCRfish ÷ FCRplant, control FCRplant, control = larger of (DMp÷DMf)(Np÷Nf)÷NUE and (DMp÷DMf)(Pp÷Pf)÷PUE PFRMo = Woplant ÷ Wofish PFRMh = (Woplant + Gplant) ÷ (Wofish + Gfish) PFRR = PFRMh × priceplant ÷ pricefish

Planning (our rearrangement of the same definitions):

Plant production = fish production × PFRM Grow area = plant production ÷ yield per m² Feed = fish production × FCRfish FCRplant = FCRfish ÷ PFRM Loading rate = feed (g) ÷ grow area ÷ days

Example

Over 35 days a 10 m² lettuce raft gains 35 kg (fresh) while the fish in a 1 m³ tank gain 10.5 kg on 15.75 kg of feed. PFRM = 35 ÷ 10.5 = 3.33 kg of plants per kg of fish (0.3 kg of fish per kg of plants). The fish FCR is 15.75 ÷ 10.5 = 1.5 and the plant FCR is 15.75 ÷ 35 = 0.45; 1.5 ÷ 0.45 = 3.33, the same as PFRM. The plants gained 3.5 kg/m², the fish 10.5 kg per m³ of tank, there are 10 m² of grow area per m³ of tank, and the loading rate is 15 750 g ÷ 10 m² ÷ 35 days = 45 g/m²/day, inside the FAO leafy-green range of 40–50 [3]. No dry matter is entered by default, so the dry PFRM is blank.

Theory: for lettuce with warm-water feed, phosphorus is limiting and the theoretical FCRplant is (4.37 ÷ 91.5) × (0.76 ÷ 1.02) ÷ 0.151 = 0.236 [1]. With this system's own fish FCR of 1.5 the theoretical PFRM is 1.5 ÷ 0.236 = 6.36 (5.94 with the paper's 1.40, which Table 3 rounds to 6.0). The measured 3.33 is 0.52 times that: the lettuce grew about half as much per kg of fish as average nutrient retention would allow. If the fish were stocked at 5 kg and the lettuce at 1 kg, the standing ratio would go from 1 ÷ 5 = 0.2 at stocking to 36 ÷ 15.5 = 2.32 at harvest; at $8/kg for lettuce and $10/kg for fish, PFRR = 2.32 × 8 ÷ 10 = 1.86, so the plants would bring in nearly twice the revenue of the fish.

Plan mode default: to grow 100 kg of fish in 35 days at a PFRM of 3.5 you get 100 × 3.5 = 350 kg of plants. At a yield of 3.5 kg/m² over the period that needs 350 ÷ 3.5 = 100 m². At a fish FCR of 1.5 you feed 150 kg, the implied FCRplant is 1.5 ÷ 3.5 = 0.43, and the loading rate is 150 000 g ÷ 100 m² ÷ 35 days = 42.9 g/m²/day.

Limitations and assumptions

  • Same period. Plant and fish gains must cover the same days. A fish grow-out usually lasts months while a lettuce cycle lasts weeks, so add up all plant harvests in the fish period.
  • Fresh weight differs by crop. Lettuce is mostly water, fruiting crops less so, and fish are much drier than lettuce. A fresh-weight PFRM is only comparable between similar crops; use the dry-weight version to compare across crops.
  • The theory uses averages. The theoretical PFRM depends on the fish FCR, feed and crop composition, and above all on how much N and P the plants retain, which varies widely between studies [1]. It ignores N and P in the make-up water, and other elements (potassium, calcium, iron) can limit growth first.
  • Supplements and solids. Added iron, potassium or calcium, and solids removed from the system, change how much plant growth each kilogram of fish brings [3, 4].
  • Plan mode is linear. It assumes the PFRM and yield you enter stay the same at the new scale.
  • Loading-rate ranges are rules of thumb for warm-water fish on a ~32% protein feed [2, 3, 4].
