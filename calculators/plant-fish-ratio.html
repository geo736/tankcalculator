How the plant-fish mass ratio calculator works

Aquaponic systems are often described by how many fish they hold per plant or per square metre, but that says little about what the system produces. Colt and Semmens (2022) proposed the plant-fish mass ratio (PFRM): plant mass divided by fish mass, in kg per kg [1]. A plant-fish ratio can be based on numbers of plants and fish or on mass; PFRM is the mass version [1]. A 2026 lettuce–tilapia study used an experimental version, PFRMexp, together with the plant feed conversion ratio to judge six feed loading rates [2].

This page computes PFRM from the weight gained by plants and fish over the same period: PFRM = plant gain ÷ fish gain. A PFRM of 3 means the system grew 3 kg of plants for every kilogram of fish. If you add the feed, it also gives the two feed conversion ratios and checks that their ratio equals PFRM. Add the grow area, tank volume and period for production per area and per volume and the feed loading rate.

In Plan mode you enter a fish production target, a PFRM and the plant yield your grow beds give per m² over the same period. The page works out the plant production (fish × PFRM), the grow area that needs (plants ÷ yield) and, with a fish FCR, the feed and the loading rate.

Why it matters

Every gram of plant growth in aquaponics comes from nutrients in the fish feed, so plant and fish production are linked through the feed. PFRM puts that link into one number that can be compared between systems, crops and seasons. Colt and Semmens recommended reporting it in aquaponic research [1]; their abstract notes that estimating theoretical FCRplant and PFRM values needs basic information on the composition of the feed and the plants (dry matter, nitrogen and phosphorus) and on nutrient retention [1]. On a farm, PFRM answers a planning question directly: if you want to sell 1 tonne of fish a year, about how much produce, and how much grow area, comes with it?

What is and is not confirmed from the source paper

  • Confirmed (abstract, conference summaries and search snippets of [1]): PFRM is a plant-to-fish mass ratio in kg/kg, as opposed to a ratio of numbers; FCRplant = feed supplied ÷ increase in plant weight; theoretical values are estimated from feed and plant composition and nutrient retention.
  • Not confirmed: we could not read the full text. We could not confirm whether the paper's PFRM uses mass gained over a period or standing mass at a point in time (a snippet shows a time-indexed form, PFRMt = Wplant ÷ Wfish), whether it uses fresh or dry weight, or any numeric theoretical or typical range. This page therefore uses the plain gain ratio on a fresh-weight basis, gives an optional dry-weight version, and does not rate your PFRM as high or low.

Formulas

With Gplant and Gfish = fresh weight gained over the period, F = feed fed in the same period, dp and df = dry matter (%):

PFRM = Gplant ÷ Gfish (kg plant per kg fish) PFRMdry = (Gplant × dp) ÷ (Gfish × df) FCRfish = F ÷ Gfish FCRplant = F ÷ Gplant FCRfish ÷ FCRplant = (F ÷ Gfish) ÷ (F ÷ Gplant) = Gplant ÷ Gfish = PFRM

The last line is our own algebra, not a formula quoted from the paper: when both FCRs use the same feed over the same period, the feed cancels and their ratio is exactly the gain-based PFRM. It holds only on the same weight basis (both fresh or both dry). It is useful in practice: at a given fish FCR, raising PFRM is the same thing as lowering FCRplant.

Planning (our rearrangement of the same definitions):

Plant production = fish production × PFRM Grow area = plant production ÷ yield per m² Feed = fish production × FCRfish FCRplant = FCRfish ÷ PFRM Loading rate = feed (g) ÷ grow area ÷ days

Example

Over 35 days a 10 m² lettuce raft gains 35 kg (fresh) while the fish in a 1 m³ tank gain 10.5 kg on 15.75 kg of feed. PFRM = 35 ÷ 10.5 = 3.33 kg of plants per kg of fish (0.3 kg of fish per kg of plants). The fish FCR is 15.75 ÷ 10.5 = 1.5 and the plant FCR is 15.75 ÷ 35 = 0.45; 1.5 ÷ 0.45 = 3.33, the same as PFRM. The plants gained 3.5 kg/m², the fish 10.5 kg per m³ of tank, there are 10 m² of grow area per m³ of tank, and the loading rate is 15 750 g ÷ 10 m² ÷ 35 days = 45 g/m²/day, inside the FAO leafy-green range of 40–50 [3]. No dry matter is entered by default, so the dry PFRM is blank.

Plan mode default: to grow 100 kg of fish in 35 days at a PFRM of 3.5 you get 100 × 3.5 = 350 kg of plants. At a yield of 3.5 kg/m² over the period that needs 350 ÷ 3.5 = 100 m². At a fish FCR of 1.5 you feed 150 kg, the implied FCRplant is 1.5 ÷ 3.5 = 0.43, and the loading rate is 150 000 g ÷ 100 m² ÷ 35 days = 42.9 g/m²/day.

Limitations and assumptions

  • Same period. Plant and fish gains must cover the same days. A fish grow-out usually lasts months while a lettuce cycle lasts weeks, so add up all plant harvests in the fish period.
  • Fresh weight differs by crop. Lettuce is mostly water, fruiting crops less so, and fish are much drier than lettuce. A fresh-weight PFRM is only comparable between similar crops; use the dry-weight version to compare across crops.
  • No standard range shown. We could not confirm the paper's theoretical PFRM range, so the page does not judge your value. It depends on feed protein and phosphorus, the crop's nutrient content and how well the system retains nutrients [1].
  • Supplements and solids. Added iron, potassium or calcium, and solids removed from the system, change how much plant growth each kilogram of fish brings [3, 4].
  • Plan mode is linear. It assumes the PFRM and yield you enter stay the same at the new scale.
  • Loading-rate ranges are rules of thumb for warm-water fish on a ~32% protein feed [2, 3, 4].
