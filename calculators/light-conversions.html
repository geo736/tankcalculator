How the light conversion calculator works

Light can be measured in two different ways. Photometric units (lux and foot-candles) count light the way the human eye sees it. The eye is most sensitive to green-yellow light around 555 nm and barely sees deep blue or deep red. Quantum units (PPFD, µmol/m²/s) count every photon between 400 and 700 nm equally, which is much closer to how photosynthesis works. Radiometric units (W/m²) measure the energy those photons carry.

Lux and foot-candles convert to each other exactly. Converting either one to PPFD or W/m², or back, needs a factor that depends on the lamp's spectrum. The calculator takes your reading in any of the four units, converts it to PPFD with the factor for the light source you pick, and then works out the other units from that PPFD.

Lux is weighted for human vision, so it is only an approximation for plants. Two lamps that read the same lux can give quite different PPFD. A quantum (PAR) meter measures PPFD directly and is always the better tool for grow lights.

Why it matters in hydroponics and aquaponics

  • Many growers own a cheap lux meter or phone app, while crop guidance and grow-light datasheets use PPFD and DLI.
  • Greenhouse and shade-cloth guides are often written in foot-candles, while research papers use µmol/m²/s.
  • Converting a reading to DLI shows whether the plants in your rafts, NFT channels or media beds are getting enough light for the crop, or whether you are paying for light they cannot use.

Formulas

Lux and foot-candles (exact). One foot-candle is one lumen per square foot, and 1 ft² = 0.09290304 m²:

1 fc = 1 lm/ft² = 1 ÷ 0.09290304 lx = 10.7639 lx lux = fc × 10.7639

Lux to PPFD and W/m² PAR (approximate, depends on the light source). flux is µmol/m²/s per lux and fW is µmol per joule of 400–700 nm radiation:

PPFD (µmol/m²/s) = lux × flux lux = PPFD ÷ flux PPFD = W/m² (PAR) × fW W/m² (PAR) = PPFD ÷ fW

Daily light integral for a light that stays at the same level for h hours:

DLI (mol/m²/day) = PPFD × h × 3600 s/h ÷ 1,000,000 µmol/mol = PPFD × h × 0.0036

The LED lux factors here come from Sharakshane's (2018) regression for phosphor-white LEDs, which predicts the photon flux per 1000 lumens from colour temperature (CCT) and colour rendering index (Ra):

PPFD per klx ≈ −48 + 0.0005·CCT + 12000/CCT + 0.4·Ra + 2000/Ra 3000 K, Ra 80: −48 + 1.5 + 4.0 + 32 + 25 = 14.5 → flux = 0.0145 5000 K, Ra 80: −48 + 2.5 + 2.4 + 32 + 25 = 13.9 → flux = 0.0139

Sharakshane reports that white LEDs fall within about 7% of 15 µmol/m²/s per 1000 lux. High-CRI (90+) and very warm LEDs give more photons per lux, up to about 16. †There is no published standard W/m² factor for white LEDs. The values shown are our estimates from the photon energy of typical phosphor-white spectra (E = hc/λ, so µmol/J ≈ mean wavelength in nm ÷ 119.6). They are accurate to a few percent at best. If your fixture's datasheet gives PPFD per lux or a spectrum, enter its factors with Custom factors.

Do not use a lux meter under red/blue (“blurple”) LEDs. The eye, and so a lux meter, barely responds to deep red (660 nm) and royal blue (450 nm) light, so the reading badly underestimates PPFD. No single factor fixes that.

Typical light levels (rules of thumb)

The range bar in the visualization converts DLI targets into the PPFD needed at your photoperiod (PPFD = DLI ÷ (h × 0.0036)). These are rules of thumb. Example

A lux meter reads 20,000 lx at canopy height under warm white LED fixtures, and the lights run 16 hours a day:

PPFD = 20,000 × 0.0145 = 290 µmol/m²/s fc = 20,000 ÷ 10.7639 = 1,858 fc W/m² = 290 ÷ 4.9 = 59.2 W/m² (PAR) DLI = 290 × 16 × 0.0036 = 16.7 mol/m²/day

A DLI of 16.7 falls in the leafy greens range (12–17) and the lower end of the fruiting crop range (15–30). It is above the seedling range, so seedlings could be grown with less light or fewer hours. The same 20,000 lx from sunlight would be 20,000 × 0.0185 = 370 µmol/m²/s, and from an HPS lamp only 20,000 × 0.0122 = 244 µmol/m²/s. That spread is why the light source matters.

Limitations and assumptions

  • Conversions are approximate. Even for the same lamp type, real spectra differ between brands, ages and dimming levels. Expect errors of 5–10% or more from any lux-to-PPFD conversion.
  • W/m² means PAR-band W/m² only. A pyranometer or weather-station “solar radiation” value covers 300–3000 nm and is not the same thing. Roughly half of solar energy falls in the 400–700 nm band.
  • DLI assumes constant light. Sunlight rises and falls during the day, so a single midday reading times the day length overstates the DLI. Use the DLI calculator for a sunny-day curve.
  • PPFD is 400–700 nm only. Far-red light (700–750 nm) from some LEDs can add to photosynthesis and changes plant shape, but it is not counted in PPFD.
  • One reading is one spot. Light falls off toward the edges of a fixture's footprint. Average several readings across the bed at canopy height.
