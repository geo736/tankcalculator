How the grow bed ratio calculator works

In a media-bed aquaponic system, water from the fish tank flows through beds filled with gravel, expanded clay or another inert medium. The bed holds the plants, traps solid waste and is home to the nitrifying bacteria that turn fish ammonia into nitrate. A quick way to check whether the beds and the tank are in balance is to compare their volumes. The calculator finds the total grow bed volume (number of beds × length × width × depth, or the volume you enter), divides it by the fish tank volume, and shows the result as 1 : R, meaning R litres of grow bed for every litre of fish tank.

It then compares the ratio with the target you choose, works out how much grow bed your tank needs at that target, how big a fish tank your beds could support, and the bed surface area involved.

Why the ratio matters

If the beds are too small for the tank, there is not enough media for biofiltration and solids capture, so the tank has to be stocked lightly. If the beds are much bigger than the tank, two problems appear: when the beds flood, the water they hold comes out of the fish tank, so the tank level can drop a long way unless the system has a sump; and the fish may not produce enough nutrients for all that growing space. Extension guides for small media-bed systems describe a 1:1 ratio for the simplest design (a fish tank, a pump and the beds) and up to about 1:2 (some guides say 1:3) when a sump tank is added, so that the fish tank level stays constant [1, 2].

Rule of thumb, not a design method

The volume ratio is a rule of thumb. It does not include how many fish are in the tank or how much they are fed, and that is what actually drives the nutrient supply and the waste load. The FAO aquaponics manual sizes a system with the feed rate ratio: grams of fish feed per day per square metre of growing area, for example 40–50 g/m²/day for leafy greens and 50–80 g/m²/day for fruiting vegetables in its small-scale design guidance [3]. Rakocy et al. give 60–100 g/m²/day for raft systems [4]. Use the separate Feed Rate to Grow Space calculator for that more precise method, and use this page as a quick check of the physical layout.

Formulas

Grow bed volume from dimensions (n beds of length L, width W, media depth D):

Vbed = n × L × W × D Abed = n × L × W

If you enter the total bed volume directly, the area comes from the depth:

Abed = Vbed ÷ D

Ratio, written fish tank : grow bed:

R = Vbed ÷ Vtank → shown as 1 : R

Sizing at a target ratio Rt:

Bed volume needed = Vtank × Rt Tank the beds support = Vbed ÷ Rt Bed area needed = Vtank × Rt ÷ D

Recommended range used on this page

Ratio (tank : bed)Meaning
below 1 : 1Beds small for the tank. Stock fish lightly or add bed or filter capacity.
1 : 1 to 1 : 2Common rule-of-thumb range for media beds [1, 2]. 1 : 1 suits a simple tank-and-bed loop; above 1 : 1 a sump is usually added.
1 : 2 to 1 : 3Possible with a sump [2], but check that the fish feed rate can supply that much growing area.
above 1 : 3Beds very large for the tank. Nutrients are likely to run short unless the fish are stocked and fed heavily.

Example

A 1,000 L fish tank feeds two media beds, each 200 cm × 100 cm and filled 30 cm deep. Each bed holds 2.0 × 1.0 × 0.30 = 0.60 m³, so the two beds total 1,200 L and 4.0 m² of growing area. The ratio is 1,200 ÷ 1,000 = 1.2, shown as 1 : 1.2, which is inside the 1 : 1 to 1 : 2 range. At a 1 : 1 target the tank needs 1,000 L of bed (3.33 m² at 30 cm deep), so the beds are 200 L larger than needed, and on their own they could support a 1,200 L fish tank.

For comparison, the FAO manual's small-scale media bed unit pairs 1,000 L of fish tank with 3 m² of growing space [3]. At 30 cm of media that is about 900 L of bed, close to 1 : 1.

Limitations and assumptions

  • Gross volume. The ratio uses the full bed volume including the media, as the rule of thumb does. The media takes up much of that space, so the water a bed holds when flooded is much less than its volume.
  • Media beds only. The volume ratio is used for media (flood-and-drain or constant-flow) beds. Deep water culture and NFT systems are sized by area and feed rate instead.
  • Fish load is not included. Two tanks of the same size with very different stocking densities need very different growing areas. Check the feed rate ratio before building.
  • Depth. FAO recommends media beds about 30 cm deep so there is room for a dry surface layer, a root zone and a lower zone where solids collect [3]. Much shallower beds give less biofiltration per square metre.
