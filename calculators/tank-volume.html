How the tank volume calculator works

The calculator finds two volumes: the total capacity of the tank when it is full to the top, and the water volume at the depth you enter. Both come from standard geometry. Each measurement can be entered in its own unit (for example the diameter in inches and the height in centimeters). Everything is converted to meters before the calculation, and the result is converted to your chosen unit, and the water weight is found by multiplying the volume by the density of the water.

Why tank volume matters

Almost every other calculation in a fish or plant system starts from the water volume. In aquaponics and recirculating aquaculture (RAS) you need it to:

  • set fish stocking density (kg of fish per m³ or per 1,000 L);
  • dose salt, buffers, medications or nutrients at the correct concentration;
  • size pumps and filters by how many times per hour the water is turned over;
  • check that a floor, stand or deck can carry the weight of a full tank.

The FAO aquaponics manual (Somerville et al., 2014) and Recirculating Aquaculture (Timmons & Ebeling, 2010) both base stocking, feeding and flow guidance on the volume of the culture tanks.

Formulas

Rectangular tank (aquariums, IBC totes, raceways):

Capacity = L × W × H Water = L × W × d

Round tank (vertical cylinder) with radius r = D ÷ 2:

Capacity = π × r² × H Water = π × r² × d

Cone-bottom tank: a cylinder on top of a cone of height hc. Depth d is measured from the tip of the cone.

Capacity = π r² Hwall + ⅓ π r² hc If d ≤ hc: Water = ⅓ π (r·d/hc)² d If d > hc: Water = ⅓ π r² hc + π r² (d − hc)

Horizontal cylinder: the water fills a circular segment of the end face, multiplied by the length L.

Segment area A = r² · arccos((r − d) / r) − (r − d) · √(2rd − d²) Water = A × L Capacity = π r² L

Water weight

Weight = Volume (L) × density (kg/L)

Freshwater at 20 °C has a density of about 0.998 kg/L (USGS). Seawater at 35 practical salinity units and 20 °C is about 1.025 kg/L (Millero & Poisson, 1981). The calculator uses these two values. Warmer water is slightly lighter and colder water slightly heavier, but the difference is less than 0.5% across normal culture temperatures.

Example

A 48 × 24 × 24 inch glass tank filled to 20 inches holds 48 × 24 × 20 = 23,040 in³ of water. Dividing by 231 in³ per gallon gives 99.7 US gallons (377.6 L). As freshwater it weighs about 377 kg (831 lb), before counting the glass, gravel and stand.

Things the calculator does not include

  • Wall thickness. Measure inside dimensions, or subtract the wall thickness from outside measurements.
  • Displacement. Gravel, media, rocks and equipment take up space. A grow bed full of expanded clay holds much less water than its volume suggests.
  • Plumbing. Water in pipes, sumps and filters adds to the system total. Add each tank separately to get the total system volume.
