How the plant FCR calculator works

Fish farmers judge feeding with the feed conversion ratio (FCR): kilograms of feed given per kilogram of fish weight gained. In aquaponics the same feed also fertilizes the plants, so Colt and Semmens (2022) proposed an FCR for the plants as well: the plant feed conversion ratio, FCRplant = feed supplied ÷ increase in plant weight [1]. It tells you how well the nutrients in the fish feed end up as plant tissue. A lower FCRplant means more plant growth from each kilogram of feed.

In Measure mode you enter the feed given to the fish during a growing period and the plant weight gained in the same period (harvest minus transplants). The page divides one by the other. If you add the grow area and the period, it also gives the feed loading rate in g of feed per m² of grow area per day and compares it with the guideline ranges used on the Feed Rate to Grow Space calculator. If you weighed the fish, it adds the ordinary fish FCR, the total food produced per kilogram of feed and the plant-fish mass ratio.

In Plan mode you enter a target plant production and an FCRplant (ideally one you measured). The page multiplies them to get the feed you will need, and with a period and an area turns that into feed per day and a loading rate.

Why it matters

Feed is the main running cost and the only real nutrient input in most aquaponic systems. The usual sizing rule, the feed rate ratio, says how much feed a square metre of plants can use (for example 40–50 g/m²/day for leafy greens) [3, 4], but it does not tell you how much the plants actually grew from it. FCRplant closes that loop. Colt and Semmens recommended that FCRplant be reported in aquaponic research [1]. Their abstract says that a theoretical range can be estimated from the composition of the feed and the plants and from nutrient (nitrogen and phosphorus) retention, and that only about half of the published experimental values they examined fell inside that range, which points to large room for improving nutrient retention in the plant part of many systems [1]. A 2026 study of Bibb lettuce with Nile tilapia used FCRplant and the plant-fish mass ratio to judge six feed loading rates (15–90 g/m²/day of a 32% protein feed) and reported that 30, 45, 60 and 75 g/m²/day were within standard operating performance for protein and energy retention, while 15 and 90 were not [2].

What is and is not confirmed from the source paper

  • Confirmed (abstract and summaries of [1]): FCRplant = amount of feed supplied ÷ increase in weight of plants; a theoretical range can be computed from feed and plant composition and N and P retention; about 50% of experimental values fell inside it.
  • Not confirmed: we could not read the full text, so this page does not show the paper's numeric theoretical range, and we could not confirm whether its tables use fresh or dry plant weight or include roots. This calculator uses fresh weight by default and gives a dry-weight version if you enter a dry-matter percentage. Compare only values on the same basis.

Formulas

With F = feed supplied over the period, H = fresh plant harvest, S = transplant weight, d = dry matter (%), T = period (days), A = grow area (m²), Gfish = fish weight gain:

Gplant = H − S FCRplant = F ÷ Gplant (kg feed per kg plant, fresh) Plant per kg feed = Gplant ÷ F = 1 ÷ FCRplant FCRplant, dry = F ÷ (Gplant × d ÷ 100) Loading rate = F (g) ÷ A ÷ T (g/m²/day) Yield = Gplant ÷ A FCRfish = F ÷ Gfish Food per kg feed = (Gplant + Gfish) ÷ F PFRM = Gplant ÷ Gfish = FCRfish ÷ FCRplant

Plan mode turns the first formula around: feed needed = target plant gain × FCRplant, feed per day = feed ÷ T, and the grow area that feed fits at the guideline ratio R is feed per day ÷ R. The last line is our own algebra: because both FCRs use the same feed, dividing them cancels the feed and leaves the plant-to-fish gain ratio.

Example

A 10 m² lettuce raft is fed from a fish tank for 35 days. The fish get 15.75 kg of feed in that time (450 g a day, which is 45 g/m²/day). You planted 1 kg of seedlings and harvested 36 kg of lettuce, so the plants gained 35 kg. FCRplant = 15.75 ÷ 35 = 0.45 kg feed per kg lettuce, or 35 ÷ 15.75 = 2.22 kg of lettuce per kg of feed. At 4.4% dry matter the dry gain is 35 × 0.044 = 1.54 kg, so the dry-weight FCRplant is 15.75 ÷ 1.54 = 10.23. The plants gained 35 ÷ 10 = 3.5 kg/m². The loading rate is 15 750 g ÷ 10 m² ÷ 35 days = 45 g/m²/day, inside the FAO leafy-green range of 40–50. The fish gained 10.5 kg, so their FCR is 15.75 ÷ 10.5 = 1.5, the system made (35 + 10.5) ÷ 15.75 = 2.89 kg of food per kg of feed, and the plant-fish mass ratio is 35 ÷ 10.5 = 3.33 (= 1.5 ÷ 0.45).

Plan mode turns this around. To grow 35 kg of lettuce in 35 days at an FCRplant of 0.45 you need 35 × 0.45 = 15.75 kg of feed, or 450 g/day. At 40–50 g/m²/day that feed fits 450 ÷ 50 = 9.0 to 450 ÷ 40 = 11.25 m² of grow area; on 10 m² the loading rate is 45 g/m²/day and the plants give 3.5 kg/m². At a fish FCR of 1.5 the fish gain 15.75 ÷ 1.5 = 10.5 kg.

Limitations and assumptions

  • Same period, same feed. Feed, plant gain and fish gain must cover the same days. Count all plants harvested in the period and the change in standing crop if beds were not empty at both ends.
  • Fresh weight varies. Fresh weight depends on water content, trimming and whether roots are included. Dry weight is a fairer basis for comparing crops.
  • No published standard range shown. The theoretical FCRplant range in [1] depends on feed protein and phosphorus, plant N and P content and nutrient retention; we could not confirm its numbers, so the page does not rate your FCRplant as good or bad. Track it over time and compare like with like.
  • Supplements. Iron, potassium or calcium added to the water also feed the plants but are not counted in F [3, 4].
  • Solids removal. Systems that remove fish solids lose part of the feed nutrients; systems with mineralization recover more. Both change FCRplant.
  • Loading-rate ranges are rules of thumb for warm-water fish on a ~32% protein feed [2, 3, 4].
