How the grow bed volume calculator works

The calculator works out four things for the plant side of your system: the gross volume of the beds or channels, the water they hold while the system runs, the planting area, and the number of plant sites that fit at the spacing you choose. Every measurement has its own unit. Everything is converted to meters before the calculation, and the results are shown in the volume and area units you pick.

How much water a bed holds depends on its type:

  • Media bed. The bed is full of gravel, lava rock or expanded clay, so water can only fill the gaps between the particles. When the bed floods to the top of the media, the water held is the bed volume times the water-filled pore space (the share of the bed that is gaps).
  • Deep water culture (DWC, raft). Plants float on foam rafts over a canal of water, so the water is simply length × width × water depth.
  • Nutrient film technique (NFT). A thin film of water runs along the bottom of each channel. The water held is the cross-section of that film times the channel length. This is small, which is why most of the water in an NFT system sits in the sump or fish tank.

This page does not tell you how much media to buy (see the Growing Media Fill calculator) or whether your beds are the right size for your fish tank (see the Grow Bed Ratio calculator).

Why it matters for aquaponics and hydroponics

  • Total system volume. Water in the beds is part of the system. Add it to the fish tank, sump and filters when you dose minerals, buffers or nutrients, or when you work out turnover.
  • Sump sizing. In a flood-and-drain media bed the water held in the bed leaves the sump each time the bed fills. In NFT, the water in the channels drains back to the sump when the pump stops. The sump must be able to hold that water without overflowing.
  • Thermal and chemical stability. DWC holds a large volume of water, which buffers temperature and water quality. NFT holds very little in the channels, so the reservoir does that job (Somerville et al., 2014; Maucieri et al., 2019).
  • Weight. Water weighs about 1 kg per liter. Deep water canals and flooded media beds are heavy, so check what your floor, stand or roof can carry.
  • Plant count. The planting area and spacing decide how many plants you can grow, and therefore how much feed and nutrient the system has to supply.

Formulas

For n beds (or canals, or channels). Bed length L, width W, media depth D, water depth d, raft thickness t, water-filled pore space φ, channel length Lc, plant spacing s.

Media bed, flooded to the top of the media:

Gross volume = L × W × D × n Water = Gross volume × φ Area = L × W × n

Deep water culture. The canal must be at least as deep as the water plus the raft:

Water = L × W × d × n Gross volume = L × W × (d + t) × n Min. canal depth = d + t Area = L × W × n

NFT channels. Film depth h; rectangular gully of inside width w and height H, or round pipe of inside radius r:

Rectangular: film area A = w × h channel area = w × H Round pipe: film area A = r² · arccos((r − h) / r) − (r − h) · √(2rh − h²) channel area = π r² Water = A × Lc × n Gross volume = channel area × Lc × n Area = Lc × channel spacing × n

The round-pipe film is a circular segment, the same geometry used for a partly filled horizontal tank.

Plant sites. Each plant gets a square of side s, so only whole plants count (⌊ ⌋ means round down):

Media bed / DWC: sites = ⌊L ÷ s⌋ × ⌊W ÷ s⌋ × n NFT: sites = ⌊Lc ÷ s⌋ × n Density = sites ÷ area (plants per m²) Water weight = Water (L) × 0.998 kg/L

On a square grid, spacing s gives at most 1 ÷ s² plants per m² (20 cm gives 25 per m²). Rounding down to whole rows means small beds come out a little lower. Staggered (offset) rows can fit a few more plants than a square grid.

Typical values (rules of thumb)

These come from the FAO aquaponics manual (Somerville et al., 2014). They are guide values, not fixed rules. The calculator shows whether your planting density falls inside the FAO range for the crop you choose.

The media presets are typical values, not exact figures, and match the Growing Media Fill calculator: expanded clay 40%, lava rock 45% and gravel 38% (gravel porosity is 25–40%; Freeze & Cherry, 1979). Pore space depends on particle size, shape and packing. To measure yours, fill a bucket of known volume with media, then measure how much water it takes to fill it to the top. That volume divided by the bucket volume is φ.

Example

Media bed (the default). One bed 120 cm long, 100 cm wide, with 30 cm of expanded clay. Gross volume = 1.2 × 1.0 × 0.3 = 0.36 m³ = 360 L. Flooded at 40% pore space it holds 360 × 0.40 = 144 L of water, which weighs 144 × 0.998 = 143.7 kg. The planting area is 1.2 × 1.0 = 1.2 m². At 20 cm lettuce spacing, ⌊120 ÷ 20⌋ × ⌊100 ÷ 20⌋ = 6 × 5 = 30 plant sites, or 25 per m², at the top of the FAO range of 20–25 heads per m². That is 144 ÷ 30 = 4.8 L of bed water per plant.

Deep water culture. One canal 240 × 120 cm with 30 cm of water and a 4 cm raft holds 2.4 × 1.2 × 0.3 = 0.864 m³ = 864 L. It needs to be at least 34 cm deep, a gross volume of 979.2 L. The raft area is 2.88 m², and at 20 cm spacing it fits 12 × 6 = 72 plants (25 per m², 12 L of water per plant).

NFT. Six rectangular channels 300 cm long, 10 cm wide and 7.5 cm high, with a 5 mm film. Each holds 0.10 × 0.005 × 3 = 0.0015 m³ = 1.5 L, so the six hold 9 L, out of a gross channel volume of 6 × 22.5 = 135 L. At 20 cm spacing each channel has ⌊300 ÷ 20⌋ = 15 sites, 90 plants in all. With the channels 20 cm apart they cover 6 × 3 × 0.2 = 3.6 m² (25 per m²), with only 0.1 L of channel water per plant.

Things the calculator does not include

  • The dry zone of a media bed. In a flood-and-drain bed the water usually stops a few centimetres below the media surface, so the top layer stays dry. The water figure assumes the bed floods to the top of the media, so the real figure is a little lower. To allow for it, enter the flooded depth as the media depth.
  • Water absorbed into the media. Expanded clay and lava rock soak up some water inside each particle. This adds weight but is not counted as free water here.
  • Raft displacement. A floating raft and the plant roots displace a small amount of water. The DWC water figure uses the water depth you measure with the rafts in place, so it is close enough for dosing.
  • NFT film depth. NFT relies on a very shallow, moving film (Cooper, 1979). Its depth changes with flow rate, slope and root mass, so the channel water is an estimate. Measure the film while the pump runs.
  • Plumbing, sumps and fish tanks. Add these separately for the total system volume.
  • Bell siphons, standpipes and media guards. These take up a little space in the bed and are ignored.
